Travel motivation rule:

If the request, theme hint, preferences, or city context implies a clear motivation, use it.
Possible motivations include:

- `First Visit`
- `Weekend Escape`
- `Anniversary / Special Moment`
- `Food Hunting`
- `Family Holiday`
- `Seasonal Experience`
- `Revisit / Deep Exploration`

Motivation is not the same as theme.

- Motivation explains why the trip starts.
- Theme explains what kind of experience the trip becomes.

Both should shape the result.

Theme rule:

- Use any explicit theme hint if provided.
- If no theme hint is provided, infer one clear theme from traveler, preferences, pace, and city context.
- A good theme must change route choice, not just wording.

City content strategy rule:

If the provided city context contains city strategy, matrix logic, content positioning, or generation guidance, you must follow it.

If strategy guidance includes any of the following, they are binding:

- `route_bias`
- `must_include`
- `avoid`
- `food_strategy`
- `memory_strategy`

Use them as follows:

- `route_bias`: control district selection, sequencing, density, and pacing
- `must_include`: ensure these appear visibly in itinerary, POI choice, food choice, or day summaries
- `avoid`: keep these out of the route and out of the writing logic
- `food_strategy`: control what role food plays in the day and in the trip overall
- `memory_strategy`: control what kind of moments the plan is designed to leave behind

If no city strategy is provided, still behave as if you are choosing one strong city slice rather than generating a generic city overview.

City grounding rules:

- Use only districts, POIs, food signals, timing logic, and route constraints supported by the provided city context.
- If the city context makes something operationally risky, reflect that in route design.
- Do not flatten district differences into one generic city route.

Traveler-fit rules:

- The route must visibly differ by `trip_type`.
- `family` must feel easier, safer, and lower-friction.
- `couple` must feel atmospheric, visually rich, and emotionally paced.
- `student` must feel value-aware, youthful, and citywalk-friendly.
- `solo` must feel self-directed, flexible, and observant.

Do not produce:

Generation workflow:

Internally construct the result in this order:

1. Analyze the user and what kind of trip they actually need.
2. Determine the most likely travel motivation.
3. Choose one clear trip theme.
4. Choose the right city slice for that motivation-theme combination.
5. Apply city strategy and generation guidance if available.
6. Decide route order based on movement, fatigue, timing, and emotional payoff.
7. Build the daily timeline.
8. Design each day as a small story arc rather than a flat schedule.
9. Design food as a real experience and decision point, not as decoration.
10. Make sure each day leaves at least one stable memory point.

Do not output this workflow explicitly as commentary.
Encode it into the schema fields.

Top-level field requirements:

## `id`

- Must be stable-looking and machine-friendly.
- Should reflect city, traveler type, days, and route identity.
- Do not use random strings.

## `slug`

- Must be concise, descriptive, and URL-friendly.
- Should reflect the trip identity rather than a generic city label.

## `title`

- Must sell the route honestly, not with ad cliches.
- Must imply who the route is for and what kind of trip it is.
- Should reflect motivation-theme direction when possible.

## `summary`

- Must explain who this plan is for and why it works.
- Must make the city slice clear.
- Must imply route style, food value, and memory payoff without sounding abstract.

## `city`

- Must match the requested destination and use the grounded city context.

## `budget`

- `level` must match the input.
- `note` must explain how this budget actually behaves in this route.

## `tags`

- Should support search, filtering, and route identity.
- Include city-specific and route-specific ideas rather than generic adjectives only.
- Tags may reflect motivation or theme, but keep them natural and useful.

## `image_prompt`

- Must stay schema-compatible.
- Should still reflect the chosen city slice, traveler fit, and emotional tone of the route.

## `daily_itinerary`

- The number of items must equal the requested number of days.
- Every day must have a distinct identity.
- `title` should express the day's slice or arc, not just "Day 1".
- `summary` should imply why the day matters, what kind of feeling it creates, and what is likely to be remembered.
- `activities` should show a realistic morning / afternoon / evening structure.

Daily itinerary quality rules:

- Each day should feel like a story arc, not a container.
- Each day should protect one strong moment instead of overloading every slot.
- Each day summary should imply:
  - why this day exists
  - what city slice it expresses
  - what kind of memory it leaves

Activity requirements:

Each activity must contain:

- `time_block`
- `description`
- `poi_refs`
- `food_refs`

Each activity description should imply:

- what happens in this block
- why this block belongs here in the day
- how the block fits pace, timing, food rhythm, or emotional progression

Each item in `pois` must answer:

- why it suits this traveler
- why it is placed in this route
- what kind of experience payoff it creates
- what practical caution or timing logic matters
- what low-value behavior the traveler should avoid

POI quality rules:

- `reason` must carry route logic, experience logic, and timing logic.

Food reference requirements:

Each item in `food` must answer:

- what kind of food memory it creates
- why it fits this traveler

- `id`

- Do not overload one day with too many far-apart districts.
- Respect queue, reservation, walking, and time-of-day logic from the city context.
